# Golden-set review worksheet

> Generated by `scripts/golden_worksheet.py`. Do not edit this file — it is
> regenerated. Your decisions go in `tests/golden/companies.json`.

Every company in the golden set is now scored, and **two of the eight**
are scored by arithmetic rather than by anyone's judgement. The other six
carry `reviewed_by: assistant-draft`: Claude read the profile and scored it
against the published rubric, at the owner's explicit instruction.

That is worth being plain about. The audit engine runs on the same model
family that wrote those expectations, so an agreement figure over those six
is not a clean accuracy measurement — it is partly a measure of a model
agreeing with itself. It is better than an unreviewed set, which measures
nothing at all, and it is weaker than an analyst. `ReviewedBy` in
`tests/golden/loader.py` keeps the distinction machine-readable so an eval
report cannot quietly drop the caveat.

## How to use it

This is now a **challenge sheet**, not a blank one. For each company below:

1. Read *what the founder submitted* and *what the platform computed*.
2. Decide what the audit **should** say. Not what it does say — what a
   founder deserves to be told, and what SACI is willing to put its name to.
3. Compare that against the proposed answer. **Where you disagree, you are
   right and the draft is wrong** — it was written without a real audit to
   check it against.
4. Edit the `expected` block shown at the end of each section, in
   `tests/golden/companies.json`, and set `"reviewed_by": "human"` on any
   company you have personally decided.

The companies marked `deterministic` need no review at all: their verdicts
come from the integrity floor and the coverage ratio, and no model output
reaches them. Spend the time on the six that say `assistant-draft`.

Score bands, not exact numbers. A model-derived dimension score is not
reproducible to the unit, so an exact expectation would fail on noise and
teach everyone to ignore the harness. Pick the range you would accept.

## The four things a verdict can be

| Level | Means | Carries a score? |
|---|---|---|
| `ready` | Meets the bar on well-evidenced dimensions. | yes |
| `not_yet` | Enough evidence to judge, and it misses the bar. | yes |
| `provisional` | A conclusion, but the evidence is thin. Never show it as a plain result. | yes |
| `insufficient_data` | No conclusion. An absence, not a failure — never render it as 'not fundable'. | no |

Two thresholds decide these mechanically, and both are worth having in mind
while you score:

- **`READY_THRESHOLD` = 70.** Mean score at or above which a
  verdict reads `ready`. Deliberately untuned — it is a round number chosen
  before any golden set existed. **This review is what tunes it.**
- **`INTEGRITY_FLOOR` = 50.** Below this `data_integrity_score`,
  no verdict is offered at all and the rubric is never called.

## What the audit grades

Rubric `v1`. The same dimensions apply to every company, so
they are listed once here rather than repeated below. Each verdict is the
mean of the dimensions in its scope that carried real evidence.

### Financial health

*Can this business fund its own operations, and for how long?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 20

- Runway is stated and supported by the submitted figures, or is explicitly unknowable from what was submitted.
- Gross margin is consistent with the business model described (a services business showing software margins needs explaining).
- Burn is trending in a direction the founder can account for.
- Any claim of profitability is corroborated by the computed figures, not only asserted.

### Unit economics

*Does each customer earn more than they cost to acquire?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 20

- LTV/CAC and CAC payback are available, or the specific missing input is named.
- Acquisition cost includes the sales and marketing effort actually used, not only paid media.
- Retention or churn evidence supports the lifetime assumed.
- Where the ratio is strong, it is checked against whether the business is simply under-investing in growth.

### Traction

*Is there verifiable evidence that customers want this?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 15

- Revenue or usage is corroborated by a source other than the founder's own narrative.
- Growth is shown over a period long enough to distinguish a trend from a single good month.
- Named customers or contracts are evidenced, not just listed.
- Pilots, letters of intent, and paying customers are distinguished from one another rather than counted together.

### Market opportunity

*Is the addressable market real and large enough to matter?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 10

- Market size is derived, not quoted as a single unsourced headline number.
- A bottom-up derivation (customers x price) is present and is reconcilable with any top-down figure given.
- The serviceable market reflects where the business can actually operate today, including regulatory and geographic limits.
- Competition is identified specifically enough to be checked.

### Team

*Can these people execute this particular plan?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 10

- The roles the plan requires are either filled or named as gaps.
- Relevant prior experience is specific and checkable, not self-described seniority.
- Founder time commitment is stated.
- Key-person risk is identified where one individual holds the relationships, the knowledge, or the credentials.

### Legal and IP

*Does the business actually own what it is selling?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 10

- The operating entity is identified and the cap table is coherent.
- IP created by founders, employees, and contractors is assigned to the company.
- Licences or regulatory permissions the business model requires are held, or their absence is flagged.
- Material contracts and any encumbrances on them are disclosed.

### Data integrity

*Do the submitted materials agree with each other?*

Scope: fundability + saleability · weight 15

- Figures repeated across documents match, or the difference is explained.
- Units and currency are consistent and plausible for the stated scale of the business.
- Dates and periods line up across the submission.
- Nothing central to the verdict rests on a single unverifiable assertion.

### Scalability

*Would more capital produce more output, or just more cost?*

Scope: fundability only · weight 15

- The constraint capital would relieve is named specifically.
- Delivery cost per additional customer is falling, flat, or rising -- and which it is, is evidenced.
- The plan for deploying new capital maps to that constraint.
- Growth does not depend on a step change the business has never demonstrated.

### Owner independence

*Does this business still work once the founder leaves?*

Scope: saleability only · weight 20

- Customer relationships sit with the company rather than with one individual.
- Day-to-day operations are documented well enough for a successor.
- Decisions that only the founder can currently make are identified.
- Any owner compensation below market is disclosed, since it flatters the margins a buyer would inherit.

### Transferability

*Can what makes this valuable actually change hands?*

Scope: saleability only · weight 15

- Contracts survive a change of control, or the ones that do not are identified.
- Systems, accounts, and data are held by the company, not by personal accounts.
- Licences and permissions are transferable or reobtainable.
- Supplier and platform dependencies are named.

### Revenue durability

*Will the revenue still be there next year?*

Scope: saleability only · weight 15

- Recurring or contracted revenue is separated from one-off sales.
- Customer concentration is quantified.
- Contract lengths and renewal behaviour are evidenced.
- Revenue dependent on a single channel, platform, or counterparty is identified.

## 6. `founder-dependent-agency`

**Why this company is in the set.** The two verdicts must be able to diverge. A profitable agency that dies without its founder is fundable-ish and clearly less saleable; if both verdicts always move together, the saleability dimensions are not doing any work. Note how little they can currently diverge: 7 of the 10 saleability dimensions are shared with fundability, and because the unweighted mean ignores `DimensionSpec.weight`, the three saleability-only dimensions carry 30% of the verdict instead of the 50% the declared weights give them. The gap below is roughly 10 points; under the declared weights it would be about twice that. The ordering is the claim and the bands are only its shadow, so this is the one fixture carrying `saleability_below_fundability`: the accuracy half must compare the two observed scores directly, not just check each against its band. The bands are also deliberately made not to overlap (46-69 against 32-45), because two overlapping bands are both satisfied by a run that scored the verdicts identically -- which is the exact failure this company exists to catch.
